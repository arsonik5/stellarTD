# Rules, not textures, make AAA ground

AAA voxel and planet ground doesn't get its look from the photoscans. It gets it from five layers stacked on top of them, and Stellar TD has only parts of two. The shipped games pick the material from data (slope, erosion, geology, soil), not from paint. They blend materials by height with a narrow transition, so stones poke through dust instead of cross-fading. They sample each texture at a ladder of scales that changes with distance. They put real geometry on the ground (pebbles, rocks, boulders) placed by density rules tied to the same data and to the surface material, so digging changes the scatter for free. And they spend real effort on occlusion and indirect light, mostly in holes and caves. Space Engineers 1 documents the cheapest working version of almost all of this: a triplanar side/top split, a texture-scale ladder of x4 per x4 distance, far texture sets and a flat far colour, pebble "foliage" per m² keyed to the material, and a bare/damaged material that dug voxels turn into. SE2 adds height-masked transitions that span several triangles, tessellation and parallax, and ray-traced GI. Star Citizen v5 and Horizon Zero Dawn add the data-layer and density-graph thinking that scales to many planets. Godot 4.7 with godot_voxel 1.7 can do everything except hardware tessellation and full ray-traced GI. The current terrain shader spends about 29 texture fetches per pixel on 2 materials and still lacks the multi-layer height blend, the distance ladder, specular occlusion and radial-safe macro variation. So the first wins are restructuring (biplanar, a 4-layer height blend, a distance ladder) rather than bigger textures. Planets aren't presets here. Each one is a weighted mix of blendable traits (airless, icy, volcanic, crystalline, desert, sulfur, life), and each trait brings its own materials, palette and catalogue of asset lines and composed features (boulder groups, crater sets, vent sets, cliff sets, landmarks). Art rules for colour harmony, composition and vistas sit on top of the physics, so a mix like a crystal moon reads as one beautiful world. The plan below is built as a data flow seed → cluster → system → planet → traits → biome → material/scatter rules, and each stage says what still falls short of the north star.

## Shipped games split into a cheap documented baseline and an expensive modern layer

**Space Engineers 1 is the only voxel game with a fully public material schema, and it's a ready blueprint for a Transvoxel planet.** Each voxel material has separate side (XZ) and top (Y) texture sets packed as ColorMetal, NormalGloss and an extension map, so it's triplanar with a distinct top projection. Tiling follows a ladder: `InitialDistance` 5 m, `InitialScale` 2, and the texture scale is multiplied by 4 each time the distance grows by 4 (`ScaleMultiplier`/`DistanceMultiplier` 4). Then come `Far1` (scale 400) and `Far2` (scale 2000) with their own optional texture sets, and `Far3` at scale 7000, where a flat `Far3Color` takes over ([SE wiki](https://spaceengineers.wiki.gg/wiki/Modding/Reference/SBC/VoxelMaterial_Definition)). The same definition carries `BareVariant` (the material under grass), `DamagedMaterial` (what drills and explosions turn it into), a `ColorKey` that tints the surface over ore, and `FoliageType` 1 = pebbles with a `FoliageDensity` per m² ([SE wiki](https://spaceengineers.wiki.gg/wiki/Modding/Reference/SBC/VoxelMaterial_Definition)). SE1 tops out at **128 active voxel materials** ([Keen support](https://support.keenswh.com/spaceengineers/pc/topic/22619/vote/add/1), user report).

**SE2 looks modern up close for three documented reasons.** Material transitions "can extend across multiple voxels and triangles" and use "a transition mask with height information" instead of SE1's one-triangle vertex blend ([SteamDB patch notes](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/19659200), snippet only). The VRAGE3 pipeline has "tessellated terrain, and parallax mapping" ([SE2 game guide](https://2.spaceengineersgame.com/game-guide/)) and ray-traced dynamic GI with cascaded sun shadows stabilised for planet distances ([Hloušek, 2023](https://blog.marekrosa.org/2023/04/guest-post-jan-hlousek-vrage3/)). Its material system has parallax, alpha-cutout, triplanar and transparent PBR variants, and Keen mentions "new tiling methods" without saying what they are ([Hloušek, 2023](https://blog.marekrosa.org/2023/04/guest-post-jan-hlousek-vrage3/)).

**Star Citizen v5 and Horizon Zero Dawn show how this scales past one hand-tuned planet.** SC v5 assigns and blends terrain textures from planet data layers (temperature, humidity, geology, soil type and depth, sunlight exposure, slope aspect), "tiled without visible repetition". It places rocks and debris by erosion simulation, so loose rock gathers under cliffs, and it colours distant terrain with data about flora not yet streamed in, so the ground doesn't change colour at the scatter cull line ([SC Wiki v5](https://starcitizen.tools/Planet_Tech_v5), [The Impound](https://theimpound.com/pages/star-citizen-starchitect-planet-tech-v5)). HZD never places objects directly. Node graphs output one density map per asset from world maps (height, erosion wear/flow/deposition, cavity, variance), multiply masks, remap with curves, and a precomputed point pattern with per-point thresholds turns density into deterministic, footprint-spaced positions. It ran **100,000+ objects of 500+ asset types for about 250 µs of GPU time** ([Guerrilla GDC 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf), [slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gpubased-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn/72897079)).

The rest is thin. NMS's pipeline (voxels → polygonize → texture → populate) is public at talk level only ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024265/Continuous-World-Generation-in-No)). Enshrouded invested in dynamic diffuse and specular GI through terrain voxels ([Khronos GPC listing](https://www.khronos.org/events/graphics-programming-conference)). Astroneer has no textures at all ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astroneer)). No source this round confirmed that any of these games uses photogrammetry, stochastic tiling, micro-shadowing or specular occlusion on terrain, and no one published ms budgets. Treat those as standard techniques from the wider industry, not as copied features.

## The shader's biggest gains come from restructuring, not resolution

The current `voxel_terrain_material.gdshader` has the right foundations: whiteout triplanar normals, block-relative UVs that avoid 20 km float precision loss, a face-normal veto against barcode streaks, and top-2 material selection that doesn't trust slot order. But counting its fetches shows where the budget goes. Each `sample_planes` call is 6 fetches (3 projections x albedo + normal). The anti-tile second sample doubles that to 12 per material, and two materials give 24. The tile-mix mask, the blend noise and the macro sample add 5, so **about 29 fetches per pixel for 2 materials**. Two lines also break the project's radial-gravity rule: the macro variation samples `v_world_pos.xz` (Y-up, and it's the full 20 km world position, so precision loss too), and the anti-tile mask adds `big.xz * 0.31`. On a sphere both stretch into streaks toward the poles of the Y axis.

**Biplanar mapping is the single largest saving.** It samples only the two dominant projections, picked from the normal, with hand-supplied gradients through `textureGrad` ([iquilezles.org](https://iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar), [Babylon.js BiPlanarBlock](https://app.unpkg.com/@babylonjs/core@8.12.1/files/Materials/Node/Blocks/biPlanarBlock.js)). The minor plane's weight is remapped to zero at the cube diagonal, so the result is close to triplanar with slight artifacts near (±1,±1,±1). That cuts fetches by a third and removes the 3-projection band entirely. The weight remap and the major/median axis selection here are recalled from IQ's article, which didn't load this session, so check them against the page before shipping.

**Multi-layer height blending is nearly free and is what SE2's transitions look like.** The production formula (Unity TerrainLit, HDRP LayeredLit, and a WGSL port) is: add the layer's weight to its height, take the max, keep only layers within a transition width of the max, multiply by the original weights, renormalise ([Unity SRP source](https://plastichub.unity.cn/unity-tech-cn/ScriptableRenderPipeline/src/commit/654f18cb-413f-4e79-92bf-227477626df1...com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/HDRP/Material/TerrainLit/TerrainLitSplatCommon.hlsl), [plumesplat](https://docs.rs/crate/plumesplat/0.1.1/source/src/shaders/blending.wgsl)):

```glsl
vec4 h = vec4(hA, hB, hC, hD) * u_height_scale4 + w;   // per-material scale from the palette
float m = max(max(h.x, h.y), max(h.z, h.w));
vec4 b = max(h - m + u_transition, 0.0) * w;           // u_transition ~0.05-0.2
b /= max(b.x + b.y + b.z + b.w, 1e-5);
```

It works for all 4 MIXEL4 slots, which godot_voxel packs into `CUSTOM1.x` (indices) and `CUSTOM1.y` (weights) at 8 bits ([smooth_terrain.md](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/blob/master/doc/source/smooth_terrain.md)). The current two-material smoothstep with noise can be dropped. The rule that makes it pay: skip a layer whose *vertex* weight is under ~0.02 (a coherent branch, since weights vary per triangle), but every layer that survives must fetch its height before the final weights exist. More than 4 materials meeting in one cell can artifact ([voxel-tools docs](https://voxel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/smooth_terrain/)), so the material rules must avoid that.

**Slope, cavity, dust and wetness are ALU on data already fetched.** Slope must be `1 - dot(N, normalize(world_pos - planet_center))`, never against world Y. Fed as a bias into the rock layer's height *before* the height blend, rock comes up through dust along grain lines instead of fading in. Cavity (inverse blended height or AO) drives a dust layer of tint, roughness ~0.9 and a normal flattened toward the geometric normal. Wet planets darken albedo and lower roughness in cavities. These are engineering suggestions built on the shader research, not copied from a shipped game.

### Anti-tiling: a distance ladder everywhere, hex-tiling only near

**Mikkelsen's 2022 hex-tiling is the practical stochastic method**: 3 `SampleGrad` fetches per texture, no lookup tables, per-tile random rotation (which hides same-facing pebbles), weights sharpened with exponent γ = 7, and a contrast S-curve with r = 0.75 for albedo/roughness and r = 0.5 for normals, blended as derivatives ([JCGT 11(2)](https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/), [demo code](https://github.com/mmikk/hextile-demo)). Heitz & Neyret's histogram-preserving version needs precomputed transform textures, which means deeper integration ([Heitz](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/)). At full width hex-tiling triples the fetch count, so apply it only to the dominant layer on the dominant projection, inside about 40 m, and skip taps whose tile weight is negligible (the `lookupSkipThreshold` idea, [three-hex-tiling](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/three-hex-tiling@0.1.5/dist/index.d.ts)). No published ms numbers exist for it on any GPU.

For the far field, replace the always-on rotated second sample with **SE1's ladder**: sample at scale s and 4s, lerp across a distance band, then move to 4s and 16s further out, and end in a flat per-material far colour. That costs 2x fetches only inside the band if the branch is taken per region. Add one world-space macro variation fetch, mapped radially (triplanar or by direction from the planet centre). GPU Gems 3 recommends the same mix of projected textures and low-frequency variation for triplanar terrain ([GPU Gems 3 ch.1](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-1-generating-complex-procedural-terrains-using-gpu)).

### Parallax and micro-shadowing on triplanar, without tessellation

Godot's built-in POM needs mesh binormals and doesn't work with triplanar ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/triplanar-mapping/15093)). Godot 4 has no tessellation stage (background knowledge, flagged unverified in the notes), so SE2's tessellated displacement can't be copied. Each triplanar projection has a known tangent frame, though, so the view vector in projection space is a swizzle of the world view vector. That makes a **contact-refinement parallax** (4-8 coarse steps plus one secant step) on the dominant layer's height, dominant projection only, inside 10-20 m, affordable at roughly 8-16 extra height fetches. Marching a 4-layer height-blended surface per step is too expensive. A cheaper depth cue is a height micro-shadow: one extra height fetch offset toward the sun in the projection plane, darkening where the neighbour stands taller. Community Godot POM shaders with self-shadowing exist as references ([godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/parallax-occlusion-mapping-with-self-shadowing/)). Metre-scale relief belongs in the SDF before meshing, since 1 m Transvoxel vertices can't carry pebble detail and vertex displacement cracks LOD seams unless applied after `get_transvoxel_position()` ([smooth_terrain.md](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/blob/master/doc/source/smooth_terrain.md)).

### Occlusion is the cheapest "photoscanned" cue

Specular occlusion generalises AO to the specular lobe ([Jimenez et al., Activision](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/Practical_Real_Time_Strategies_for_Accurate_Indirect_Occlusion_NEW%20VERSION_COLOR.pdf)). HDRP derives it from AO by default ([Unity SRP](https://plastichub.unity.cn/unity-tech-cn/ScriptableRenderPipeline/commit/05c7d851-0de0-4f49-8c9f-043ed3b72b51)). Marmoset's horizon fade, `h = clamp(1 + 1.3 * dot(N_mapped, R), 0, 1); h *= h;`, stops normal-mapped reflections from pointing under the surface ([Substance lib-pbr](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d-painter/using/scripting-and-development/shader-api-reference/libraries-shader-api/lib-pbr-shader-api)). In Godot both can be written into `SPECULAR` without a custom `light()`. On an airless world almost all light is direct sun, and Godot SSAO defaults to `light_affect` 0.0 (ambient only) ([Godot Environment](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_environment.html)), so SSAO barely shows in sunlight. Raise `ssao_light_affect` a little and keep `AO_LIGHT_AFFECT` per material. Density-field AO, counting solid vs. air around each vertex ([jedjoud10](https://jedjoud10.github.io/blog/per-vertex-ao-surface-nets)), is the voxel-native answer and gets recomputed free on every remesh. Whether VoxelMesherTransvoxel can emit an extra vertex attribute for it isn't documented, so that needs a check. The fallback is sampling the existing erosion masks by direction from the planet centre. SDFGI only takes GI from Static meshes and goes stale after edits ([Godot SDFGI docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_sdfgi.html)), so it's a poor fit for a dug sphere. SSIL plus good sun shadows is the safer route, and dynamic GI in caves stays an open gap against SE2 and Enshrouded.

## Scatter has to come from the same rules as the ground, and godot_voxel already does the hard part

**VoxelInstancer is the right base and already solves dig removal.** It spawns MultiMesh items per terrain LOD, has `UP_MODE_SPHERE` for planets, filters by slope, height, noise or a `VoxelGraphFunction`, and has `voxel_texture_filter_array`, which spawns only on given material indices. That last one is SE1's material-keyed foliage. Instances are removed when the ground under them is dug, and non-persistent items respawn wherever the rules still hold ([instancing.md](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/blob/master/doc/source/instancing.md), [VoxelInstanceGenerator API](https://voxel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/VoxelInstanceGenerator/)). Three limits shape the design. Higher-LOD items are less precise and can float or sink, and `snap_to_generator_sdf_enabled` ignores edited areas. VoxelInstanceGenerator isn't scriptable (issue #336), so custom rules must go through `noise_graph`, which is what `boulder_scatter.gd` already does with the talus mask. And MultiMesh LOD swaps cost some frame time (waiting on Godot PR #79833) ([instancing.md](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/blob/master/doc/source/instancing.md)).

**Stellar TD's erosion bake is already HZD's input set.** `erosion_baker.gd` outputs flow, wear, deposition and talus masks plus slope. That's the same family of maps HZD feeds its density graphs ([slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gpubased-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn/72897079)), and it matches SC v5 placing rock by erosion. The missing piece is a per-asset density rule (mask products, curves, an inverse for clearings) defined in data per biome and compiled into each item's `noise_graph`. Sizes should come from the physics of real fields. Lunar boulder counts follow a steep power law when young and drift toward exponential with age ([Ruesch et al., INAF](https://openaccess.inaf.it/bitstreams/093842b4-b20f-44e2-a7df-9c0995e976c8/download)). Linné crater's ejecta fits a cumulative power-law index of **−4.03** ([Pajola et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00590v1)). Exponents depend on the size range measured, and small bodies can fit Weibull better ([arXiv 2112.13879](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2112.13879)). Sampling scale by inverse-CDF of N(>D) ∝ D^−b, with b set by surface age in the planet profile, gives many pebbles and few boulders. Voronoi clumping (Ghost of Tsushima's grass trick, [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027214/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Procedural-Grass)) and child pebbles around each boulder give the aprons.

**Rocks must look grown into the ground, not set on it.** The shipped options are dithered depth blending, pixel depth offset and CryEngine's terrain-layer blending into objects ([godotshaders MIT shader](https://godotshaders.com/shader/terrain-mesh-blending-with-dithering/), [CryEngine docs](https://www.cryengine.com/docs/static/engines/cryengine-5/categories/23756816/pages/36340007)). In Godot Forward+ the cheapest version avoids depth reads. Each scatter item already knows its material from `voxel_texture_filter_array`, so its shader can sample that terrain layer from the same Texture2DArray at the same world UVs and blend it into the bottom 5-20 cm of the model-space height, with dust in up-facing crevices (dot with radial up). Bury each rock by roughly 10-40% of its height. That figure is background knowledge, not sourced. Turn shadows off for debris ([GeometryInstance3D docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.2/classes/class_geometryinstance3d.html)), and fade with VoxelInstancer's `fading_enabled` shader parameter or a dither discard rather than Godot's alpha fade, which forces the transparent pass ([visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html)).

**Fresh digs need a system the instancer doesn't have.** Removal is free, but no source documents rubble spawning on new faces. Have `terrain_drill.gd` paint a damaged/bare material index on the bite (SE1's `DamagedMaterial`, through VoxelTool texture paint), so material-filtered pebbles respawn on cut faces under the normal rules. Spawn a small deterministic rubble MultiMesh at the foot of each new steep face, seeded from the edit record, so saves store edits rather than instances. Only `VoxelStreamSQLite` supports persistent instances ([instancing.md](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/blob/master/doc/source/instancing.md)).

**Decals are for unique marks, not ground cover.** Forward+ clusters decals, sharing a default cap of 512 elements with lights and probes, and cost scales mostly with screen coverage. Decals can't do parallax or custom shaders ([Godot decals](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/3d/using_decals.html)). Use them for drill scorch, footprints and crash marks, oriented to radial down. Dust drifts, crack fields and ejecta rays belong in the terrain rules.

## Planets are blends of traits, and each trait brings its own asset lines

Uniform scatter is the reason procedural ground looks procedural. The fix is the same one HZD uses for forests, applied to geology: every object exists for a reason that you can read off the ground, and objects come in composed groups (a boulder with its apron, a crater with its rim blocks and rays, a vent with its stained crust) rather than one density each. The catalogue below is a design, built from general geology and ecology plus the sourced placement models above (HZD density graphs, SC v5 erosion placement, lunar size laws). No source in this research documents a shipped game's full asset catalogue, so treat the rows as a spec to test in the lab, not as copied practice.

### Traits replace planet presets

A planet isn't "the icy type". It's a weighted mix of **traits**, picked from the seed and from the body's physical facts: distance to the star, mass, atmosphere and age, which the system generator already knows. A crystal moon is `airless 0.7 + crystalline 0.5`. An icy moon with cryovolcanoes is `icy 0.8 + volcanic 0.3`. Each trait is a data resource that declares what it contributes and how it combines:

| Trait field | What it holds | Combine rule |
|---|---|---|
| `requires` / `excludes` / `affinity` | e.g. `life` requires an atmosphere or subsurface water; `airless` excludes `wind` lines; `volcanic` has affinity with `sulfur` | the generator samples traits by weight, rejects excluded pairs, boosts affine ones |
| `spatial_mask` | where the trait shows: global, regional noise, crater floors, low altitude, high latitude, near faults, cold traps (permanently shadowed), along flow channels | traits mix *by region*, not as an average everywhere, so a crystal moon is a grey moon with crystal fields in certain craters, not a slightly crystal grey everywhere |
| `materials` | library entries for surface, strata bands and ores, each with a priority | the per-planet palette of 16 MIXEL4 slots is shared out by trait weight; low-weight traits get 1-2 slots |
| `material_mode` | `replace` (sulfur crust replaces regolith inside its mask), `overlay` (frost on top by slope/shadow in the shader), `modulate` (tint and roughness shift) | resolved per point before the height blend |
| `asset_lines` | the generators and scans below, each with its own density rule | lines add; shared-footprint lines use HZD's two-sided threshold so one point picks one asset ([slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/gpubased-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn/72897079)) |
| `palette` | hue family, value range, saturation budget, emissive allowance | harmony rules below |
| `landmarks` | hero features this trait can supply (a crystal arch, a cryo-geyser, a basalt-column cliff) | per-planet landmark budget, placed by the vista rules below |

Each asset line and material carries a `traits` list with weights (crystal clusters: `crystalline 1.0`; frost: `icy 1.0, airless-cold-trap 0.6`), so a line spawns only where its traits are active, scaled by their local mask value. Transitions between trait regions are their own lines (ecotones): the edge of a sulfur field has stained gravel and broken crust before the bright yellow starts. That's what makes a mix read as one world rather than two textures meeting.

### Beauty is a separate goal from plausibility

Physically plausible ground can still be ugly, and the user wants it beautiful, so the generator needs art rules that sit on top of the physics. These are design rules, not sourced findings.

**Colour harmony between mixed traits.** Pick the planet's base hue from the star colour and the dominant trait, then constrain each secondary trait's accent to analogous or complementary hues in a perceptual space (OKLCH). Give the planet a *saturation budget*: one accent trait may be saturated (the crystals, the sulfur, the alien flora) while the rest stay muted, and the base ground keeps a narrow value range so the accent reads. Emissive crystal or lava light counts against the same budget. Generated palettes go through the same check as hand-made ones: a thumbnail at 64 px must still show a clear base, mid and accent.

**Composition at three distances.** Near (0-10 m): texture, pebbles, one or two interesting rocks per view, no empty bands. Mid (10-200 m): clusters with a focal object and a quieter surround, never even spacing. Far (200 m+): silhouettes, which means crater rims, outcrops, ledges and landmarks that break the horizon. Density rules get a "rest" term, a low-frequency mask that leaves bare ground between clusters on purpose, because contrast between busy and empty is what makes the busy part look designed.

**Landmarks and vistas.** Each planet gets a budget of hero features (for example 1 per ~4 km² near the base and a handful planet-wide) from its traits' landmark lists. They're placed where they'll be seen: on crater rims and ridge lines visible from low ground, at the end of valleys, against the sunrise direction for the planet's spin. Each one gets a dressed surround (its own apron, transition and debris), and they're deterministic from the seed so players can tell each other where they are.

### Composed features are the unit, not single assets

Each catalogue row also belongs to one or more *compositions*, which are what the density graph actually places. A **boulder group** is one large scanned boulder, 2-5 smaller ones on the downhill side, a pebble apron and a dust shadow on the lee side. A **crater set** is a rim SDF, rim blocks, a ray pattern, a floor with dust ponds and, on crystal or icy planets, the trait's feature in the cold or sheltered floor. A **vent set** is a cone, a stain ring, a mat ring if life is on, debris and particles. A **cliff set** is a strata ledge face, talus apron, outcrops on top and lichen on the stable side. A **trait edge** is the ecotone strip between two trait masks, with mixed debris from both. In godot_voxel each composition becomes one parent density rule (HZD-style graph compiled to the item's `noise_graph`) plus child items placed around each parent instance. VoxelInstanceGenerator can't do parent-child placement natively ([issue #336](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/issues?q=instancer)), so children need either a correlated mask (same graph, offset and blurred) or a small runtime placer that reads parent positions per chunk. That's a probe to run in stage 4.

## A staged plan built as seed → cluster → system → planet → traits → biome → rules

The architecture rule for every stage: nothing in the shader or scatter knows which planet it's on. A global **material library** (an extension of the existing `SurfaceStyle`: texture set, tint, roughness, height scale/offset, far colour, bare/damaged variant, ore `ColorKey`, scatter set) feeds a per-planet **palette** of ≤16 entries. The seed picks a weighted trait mix for each planet from its physical facts, the traits share out the palette slots and asset lines by weight and mask, and the Texture2DArray and `pal_*` uniforms are built at load. That matches SE1's library-of-128 against a per-mesh limit, and SC v5's choice of material from data layers. Biomes (`Biome.gd` already selects by temperature and roughness) gain surface rules (which palette entry wins at which slope, deposition, wear and talus) and scatter rules (asset, density graph, footprint, size exponent). Strata are depth bands below the surface (layer thickness and warp from the planet's geology age), so a dig reveals a sequence. No shipped game documented strata, so this is new design.

Every stage ends with the same two gates: the physics check (does this object have a reason to be here) and the beauty check (does the shot set look like a render someone composed). Stages 2 and 4 carry most of the visual gain. Stage 2 because height blending and the ladder change every pixel. Stage 4 because geometry silhouettes are what a heightfield can't fake, which `boulder_scatter.gd`'s own header already argues. Stage 5 is where Godot falls furthest behind SE2: without tessellation and ray-traced GI, dug holes will read flatter than VRAGE3's, and parallax plus density AO only narrows the gap.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go. The instinct is to buy fidelity with sharper scans and bigger arrays, but every shipped system that looks good spends its effort on *decisions*: which material, at which slope, at which depth, with which rocks on top, at which scale for this distance. Those decisions live in data that Stellar TD already half-has. The GPU erosion bake produces exactly the masks HZD and Star Citizen use, `Biome` already selects by climate, and VoxelInstancer already removes and respawns scatter by rule. So the project's real asset is the rule layer, and making it planet-agnostic now is what makes a seeded cluster possible later. A hand-tuned moon would have to be torn up for planet two. Traits push the same idea one step further. Once materials, asset lines and palettes declare which traits they belong to, the catalogue grows sideways: every new kit (a crystal set, an ice set) multiplies the number of believable planets instead of adding one more preset. The limit on variety becomes the number of good kits, and the limit on beauty becomes the harmony and composition rules, both of which can be judged in the lab shot set.

Three things remain unknown and should be settled early by probes, not by reading: whether VoxelMesherTransvoxel can carry an extra per-vertex channel (AO, curvature, freshness), how `min/max_height` behaves under `UP_MODE_SPHERE`, and what the restructured shader actually costs in ms on the target GPU. The two hard ceilings, no tessellation and no dynamic GI that bounces off edited voxels, mean "SE2 up close" in Godot 4.7 is reachable for open ground and boulder fields. It won't be fully reached inside freshly dug caves without custom GI work beyond this plan.
