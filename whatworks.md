# What works

Validated patterns worth reusing as-is rather than re-deriving. Append a new entry whenever an approach is built and confirmed working (headless test and/or the user's playtest) — especially anything involving the `godot_voxel` GDExtension, since correct usage isn't always obvious from its docs.

## Radiation signature + scout-ship threat (Scope 1 of `radiation-threat-design.md`)

`RadiationSignature` autoload (`scripts/radiation_signature.gd`) derives a signature value from `Inventory.get_count("ore_crystal")` and exposes a `Tier` enum (None/Low/High) via `tier_changed` — no polling needed elsewhere, everything reacts to the signal. `ThreatDirector` (`scripts/world/threat_director.gd`, one inline node in `world.tscn` wired to `HomePlanet` from `world.gd::_ready()`) listens for the Low-tier transition and spawns one `EnemyScoutShip` with a cooldown gate. The scout is targetable by the existing turret system with **zero turret-side changes** — same `"enemy"` group + `take_damage()` contract as `EnemyDummy`, confirming that contract really is enough for a moving, scripted-AI enemy and not just a stationary one.

Verified via a driven test (real `await get_tree().physics_frame` loop, not manual delta calls — see `mistakes.md`): crystal count crossing 1/25 correctly flips None→Low→High→None; entering Low auto-spawns exactly one scout; a scout that reaches its flee point emits `left_system(reported)` and the director clears its `_active_scout` slot; a scout killed (`take_damage` to 0) before reaching its flee point fires `died` but never `left_system` — confirming the core design intent (shoot the scout down before it escapes and the report never goes out) actually holds in code, not just in the design doc.

## Grid-cell pipe network (`PipeNetwork` autoload) for building-to-building item transport

`scripts/pipe_network.gd` is a tiny autoload (`Dictionary[Vector3i, Node]`, `register`/`get_node_at`) that any grid-aligned building or `PipeSegment` registers into at placement time (`player.gd::_place_building()`, keyed by `Vector3i(floori(pos))`). This gives O(1) "what's in the neighboring cell" lookups for conveyor logic without needing a spatial query — cheap and correct as long as everything that should be findable is placed with `Item.force_grid_snap = true` (see `mistakes.md`-style note below: free-placed buildings straddle cell boundaries and silently break this).

**Single-mutator-per-cell invariant avoids a push/pull double-transfer race**: a `PipeSegment` only ever clears its own `_held_item_id` inside its own tick, and only ever sets it via a `pipe_accept()` call from elsewhere — pipe-to-pipe transfer is always initiated by the *upstream* segment's own `_try_advance()` (never pulled by the downstream side), while `_try_pull_from_input_side()` only reaches into a `Building` (safe, since only the puller mutates the building's inventory) and does nothing when the upstream neighbor is pipe-like. This is what makes it safe for `PipeMerger` to have 3 segments simultaneously eligible to push into it in the same physics frame.

**Merger fairness needs caller identity, not just first-come-first-served**: a plain `pipe_accept(item_id)` with no sense of *who* is calling means whichever upstream segment's `_physics_process` happens to run first in scene-tree order wins every single cycle — a fixed bias, not round-robin, and it silently starves the other two inputs. Fix: `pipe_accept(item_id, from_grid_pos)` takes the caller's cell; `PipeMerger` overrides it to only accept from its currently-favored upstream cell (a rotating cursor, advanced on every accept) unless that favored cell has nothing ready (`has_ready_item()`), in which case another caller is let through. Verified via a driven wrapper-scene test: 3 continuously-fed sources into one Merger delivered a perfectly even 14/14/14 split over an 8-second run — a naive first-come version would have shown one source dominating.

**Cardinal left/right from a 90°-stepped `forward()`**: for unit `forward = (fx, 0, fz)` (derived from `-global_transform.basis.z`, rounded — placement only ever rotates in fixed 90° steps via `KEY_R`, so this is always exact), `right = Vector3i(-fz, 0, fx)` and `left = Vector3i(fz, 0, -fx)`. Used identically by `PipeMerger._upstream_cells()` and `PipeSplitter._downstream_cells()`.

**Splitter needs no such fairness gate** — it's the sole initiator on its output side (nothing else races to push the same item out of it), so a plain rotating cursor over 3 downstream candidates, advancing only on whichever one actually accepts, is correct as-is.

## `Recipe` multi-input migration: parallel arrays over a nested sub-resource

Extending `Recipe` from single `input_id`/`input_amount` to multi-input (needed for a 3-ingredient assembly recipe) used parallel `input_ids: Array[String]` / `input_amounts: Array[int]` rather than a nested `RecipeInput` sub-resource type — hand-authoring `.tres` files is far simpler with flat typed-array literals (`Array[String](["a", "b"])`) than with `[sub_resource]` blocks. `process_recipe` pre-checks every input's availability before removing any of them (loop-check-all, then loop-remove-all) to avoid partially consuming inputs if a later ingredient turns out to be short.

## Building base class absorbs timer/auto-craft so every recipe-driven building gets it for free

Moved `_timer`/`process_recipe`/`_on_process_timeout` out of the one building that used to own them (`Processor`) and into `Building` itself, plus a new `_auto_recipes: Array[bool]` + rotating `_last_auto_index` cursor scanned each `_process()` frame. Every subclass (`Processor`, `Smelter`, `Constructor`) becomes a ~6-line script that just sets `recipes` (and any capacity overrides) in `_ready()` before calling `super._ready()` — the autocraft toggle UI in `building_ui.gd` works identically for all of them with zero building-specific code. The rotating scan cursor matters for real once a building has more than one auto-flagged recipe (confirmed via driven test: Constructor's 4 recipes with 2 flagged auto both produced output, not just recipe 0).

## Headless smoke-testing without opening the editor

`--headless --path . --quit-after 60` boots the real main scene, runs `_ready()`/a few frames, and exits — catches script parse errors, null-reference bugs, and scene-wiring mistakes with zero GUI. `--headless --path . --import --quit` forces a full rescan (resource/asset parsing, global class cache rebuild) without running the scene. Both documented in `CLAUDE.md`'s "Running / smoke-testing" section — use them before ever asking the user to manually retest something that's really just a wiring/syntax question.

For a one-off question about a specific API (e.g. "does this function actually return what I think"), a throwaway `extends SceneTree` script run via `-s some_script.gd` is fast and reliable — write it, run it, delete it. Used repeatedly this session to pin down real `VoxelTool`/`VoxelBuffer`/`VoxelMesher` behavior instead of guessing from docs (see the SDF sign convention, `MODE_ADD` vs `MODE_REMOVE`, and `VoxelFormat` depth checks below).

## Per-voxel material coloring on smooth terrain

1. `VoxelFormat.new()`, `set_channel_depth(VoxelBuffer.CHANNEL_INDICES, VoxelBuffer.DEPTH_8_BIT)`, assign to `terrain.format` — **required**, see `mistakes.md`.
2. `VoxelMesherTransvoxel.texturing_mode = VoxelMesherTransvoxel.TEXTURES_SINGLE_S4`.
3. Generator writes one material index (0-255) per solid voxel into `CHANNEL_INDICES`.
4. Shader on the terrain's material reads `CUSTOM1.x` (packed 4×8-bit indices) via `floatBitsToUint` + bitshifts to get the dominant index, and colors/textures per index. See `scripts/world/voxel_debug_material.gdshader` for the full unpack snippet and `scripts/world/world.gd` for wiring.

## Digging/building via `VoxelTool`

- `terrain.get_voxel_tool()` — cheap `RefCounted`, fine to call fresh each time rather than caching.
- Raycast against voxel terrain: `tool.raycast(world_origin, world_direction, max_distance, collision_mask)` → `VoxelRaycastResult` with `.position`/`.previous_position` (`Vector3i`, the hit voxel and the empty voxel just before it — the latter is exactly where you want to place new matter) and `.normal`. Confirmed empirically (isolated headless test, world-scaled terrain) that `from`/`dir`/`max_distance` are in **world space** and get converted internally — `.position`/`.previous_position` come back in the terrain's LOCAL (voxel-grid) space.
- Query a voxel's material before removing it: `tool.set_channel(VoxelBuffer.CHANNEL_INDICES); tool.get_voxel(pos)` — `pos` here is LOCAL space (same space `.position` above is already in, so no conversion needed when chaining off a raycast hit).
- Remove matter: `tool.set_channel(VoxelBuffer.CHANNEL_SDF); tool.set_mode(VoxelTool.MODE_REMOVE); tool.do_sphere(center, radius)`. **Unlike `raycast()`, `do_sphere()`'s `center`/`radius` are in LOCAL (voxel-grid) space too, NOT world space** — confirmed empirically (isolated headless test: a `do_sphere()` call with a world-space center only ever affected the LOCAL coordinates numerically equal to the input, never the world-equivalent local position). If the terrain node has non-1.0 `scale`, a radius meant to be a fixed world-meter size must be divided by that scale before calling `do_sphere()` — see `mistakes.md`'s 2026-09-03 "mining wouldn't dig more than 1-2 voxels" entry for the real bug this caused.
- Add matter: same but `VoxelTool.MODE_ADD`. Confirmed empirically (isolated `VoxelBuffer` test) that `MODE_ADD` makes SDF negative (solid) and `MODE_REMOVE` makes it positive (air) — not the other way around.
- Paint material onto newly-added matter: `tool.set_mode(VoxelTool.MODE_TEXTURE_PAINT); tool.set_texture_index(idx); tool.set_texture_opacity(1.0); tool.set_texture_falloff(...); tool.do_sphere(center, radius)` — a second pass after the `MODE_ADD` pass, same center/radius.

Full working example: `_dig()`/`_build()` in `scripts/player/player.gd`.

## Slot-based inventory kept backward-compatible

Rewrote `scripts/inventory.gd` from a flat `Dictionary` counter to real grid+hand slots, but kept `get_count`/`add`/`try_remove`/`changed` working exactly as before internally re-implemented over the slot array. Result: `processor.gd`, `debug_ui.gd`, and the ore/build logic in `player.gd` needed **zero changes** for a full storage-model rewrite. When replacing a data layer that other systems already depend on, preserving the existing call signatures (even if the internals change completely) avoids a cascading rewrite.

Generic slot addressing (`{"kind": "grid", "index": i}` / `{"kind": "hand", "side": "left"|"right"}`) let one `get_slot`/`set_slot`/`can_place` implementation serve both the grid and the hand slots, and let the UI's click-to-carry logic (`scripts/ui/inventory_ui.gd`) stay a single code path instead of duplicating grid-vs-hand branches.

## Building tool (KEY_C): top-down build camera + GUI, grid vs free placement, tool-gated

Satisfactory-style build tool, gated like the mining tool — `_toggle_build_mode()` refuses to *enter* unless `Inventory.has_tool_equipped(BuildingToolItem.id)` (a new equippable `Item`, `is_tool = true`), but always allows *exiting* regardless. Extended `Item` with `is_building`/`building_scene: PackedScene` so a placeable building (`data/items/basic_crafter.tres` → `scenes/processor.tscn`) is data-driven the same way `placeable_block` already is for voxel matter. What's actually placeable lives in `scripts/ui/build_ui.gd`'s `BUILDABLE_RECIPES` list (currently one entry) — adding a second building later is just another `Item`+`Recipe` pair and one more line there, `player.gd` never hardcodes which building is selected.

**Camera swap, not a menu overlay**: pressing C doesn't open a screen over the FPS view, it tweens the *same* `Camera3D` (still a child of `Player`, never reparented) from its current FPS-local transform to a top-down pose and back. This only works because the player is fully frozen for the whole duration build mode is active (`_physics_process` returns immediately at the top when `_build_mode`, skipping gravity/WASD/move_and_slide entirely) — since `Player`'s own `global_transform` never moves during that window, animating the camera's *local* transform via `create_tween().tween_property(camera, "transform", target, BUILD_CAMERA_TRANSITION_TIME)` is exactly equivalent to animating world space, no reparenting or global-transform bookkeeping needed. `Tween.TRANS_CUBIC`/`EASE_IN_OUT` for the "smoothly animated" feel.

Two states threaded through the whole system: `_build_mode` (true for the *entire* duration including both flight animations — gates player-freeze and FPS input) and `_build_view_ready` (only true once the enter-tween's `finished` signal fires — gates WASD-pan, scroll-zoom, and ghost/placement input, so nothing is interactive mid-flight). `_toggle_build_mode()` mid-transition just re-triggers the opposite tween from wherever the camera currently is — `Tween.tween_property` samples the property's live value as its start point, so no explicit "current position" bookkeeping is needed to make double-tapping C look and behave correctly.

**Returning to FPS exactly**: rather than snapshotting the camera's pre-build transform, `_fps_camera_local_transform()` reconstructs it from scratch (`Basis(Vector3.RIGHT, _pitch)` at the fixed local offset from `player.tscn`) — valid because `_pitch` is simply never touched while `_build_mode` is true (mouse-look input is gated off), so it's still exactly correct whenever the player returns.

**Top-down pan/zoom**: WASD moves a `_build_focus: Vector3` world point along the frozen player's own (frozen) forward/right basis vectors — not world-absolute directions — so panning always feels consistent with whatever way the player was originally facing. Scroll wheel adjusts `_build_zoom` (camera height above focus), clamped. Camera position each frame = `_build_focus + Vector3(0, _build_zoom, 0)`, fixed pitch (`Basis(Vector3.RIGHT, deg_to_rad(-75))`) — verified via a driven headless test that this basis is exactly right (its column vectors matched the hand-derived rotation matrix for -75° about local X).

**Ghost placement uses the mouse cursor through the top-down camera, not "look direction"** — `camera.project_ray_origin(mouse_pos)` / `project_ray_normal(mouse_pos)`, unlike the FPS dig/`_build()` raycasts which stay forward-facing from `_raycast()` and are untouched by any of this. Same grid-vs-free split as before (grid = `VoxelTool.raycast`, whole-voxel integer positions only, which *is* the snap; free = `PhysicsRayQueryParameters3D` + `direct_space_state.intersect_ray` against the terrain's generated collision, continuous float position). `BUILD_MODE_RANGE` had to jump from the FPS tools' ~6-8m to 80m once the camera itself sits 10-30m up and casts at an angle — an easy miss if reusing an existing "range" constant without checking what actually changed about the raycast's own geometry.

Build GUI (`scripts/ui/build_ui.gd` + `scenes/build_ui.tscn`) deliberately has **no full-screen background** unlike `inventory_ui.tscn` — it's a `PanelContainer` anchored to just the left edge (`anchor_right` stays 0). A full-screen `Control` defaults to `MOUSE_FILTER_STOP` and would silently eat every ground-placement click before it ever reached `_unhandled_input`; keeping the panel sized to its own content is what lets clicks outside it fall through to gameplay. Selection is exclusive-one-of-N via `toggle_mode = true` buttons whose `button_pressed` gets force-set in `_refresh_all()` after every click — simpler than fighting `ButtonGroup` for a one-off radio-button feel.

Ghost itself: a plain `MeshInstance3D` + `BoxMesh`, never an instance of the real building scene (avoids prematurely starting the Processor's `Timer` or joining the `"processor"` group), translucent `StandardMaterial3D` with `TRANSPARENCY_ALPHA` + `SHADING_MODE_UNSHADED`. `KEY_R` rotates it in fixed 90° steps via `wrapf(..., 0.0, TAU)`.

Mutual exclusion with the inventory UI: each `_toggle_*()` closes the other first if open, recursion-safe because the nested call's own guard is already false by the time it would recurse back (see `mistakes.md` history for this pattern's original derivation).

**Bootstrap trap avoided**: the world no longer spawns a `Processor` by default (removed from `world.tscn`). Its build recipe (`data/recipes/build_crafter.tres`) costs raw `ore_a`, not `refined_material` — refined_material can only be produced *by* a Processor, so pricing the first one in its own output would have made it unbuildable from a fresh save.

**How this got tested without manual play**: see the `-s`-mode autoload gotcha in `mistakes.md` — the real verification was a temporary main-scene swap that drove `_toggle_build_mode()` on the live `Player` node through a full boot, confirmed the state flags, camera basis, and mouse-mode transitions at each phase, then reverted. Worth doing again for the next input-driven state machine rather than trusting a passive `--quit-after` boot, which never exercises code that only runs in response to real input.

## Spherical voxel planet via `VoxelGeneratorScript` (SDF = distance-from-center minus surface radius)

Converting the flat-slab generator (`pos.y - height`) to a solid planet needed only two changes to the existing "custom smooth terrain generator" pattern above: `signed_distance = pos_world.distance_to(planet_center) - surface_radius` (positive outside/air, negative inside/solid — same sign convention as flat, confirmed still correct), and `surface_radius = PLANET_RADIUS + noise.get_noise_3d(dir.x, dir.y, dir.z) * AMPLITUDE` where `dir = (pos_world - planet_center).normalized()` — sampling noise on the **unit sphere direction**, not world position, is what makes the hills/craters wrap seamlessly around the whole sphere with no seam or pole-pinch (a lat/long UV approach would pinch at the poles; this doesn't, since it's just 3D noise evaluated at points on a unit sphere). Two octaves (broad `frequency~1.8` + detail `frequency~9.0`, both sampled on the same unit direction) gave visible-but-gentle terrain variation without extra generator complexity. Ore veins reused the exact same "depth below surface, noise threshold" logic unchanged, just with `depth_below_surface = surface_radius - dist_from_center` instead of `height - pos.y`. `VoxelTerrain.bounds` just needs to be a cube AABB centered on the planet center, sized `PLANET_RADIUS + amplitude + max_ore_depth + margin` per side — no LOD-mode changes needed since this project uses plain `VoxelTerrain`, not `VoxelLodTerrain`. See `scripts/world/ore_generator.gd`/`scripts/world/world.gd`.

## Radial planet gravity on a `CharacterBody3D`: track vertical speed separately from `velocity`, set `up_direction` every frame

`CharacterBody3D.up_direction` is exactly the built-in hook for non-fixed-gravity characters (it drives `is_on_floor()`/slope handling in `move_and_slide()`) — set it fresh each physics frame from `(global_position - planet_center).normalized()` before calling `move_and_slide()`. Since `velocity.y` no longer means "vertical" once up isn't always world-Y, gravity/jump state needs its own scalar (`_vertical_speed`, accumulated by `-GRAVITY*delta` and reset to 0 on landing/set to `JUMP_VELOCITY` on jump) that gets recombined into `velocity` every frame as `tangential_move_dir * SPEED + up_direction * _vertical_speed`. Confirmed via a driven headless test (see mistakes.md) that a player dropped from 5m above the surface at the spawn pole settles at the correct radius with `is_on_floor()` true, `up_direction` exactly aligned to the radial direction, and stays glued to the surface (self-correcting to within the terrain noise's amplitude) across an entire simulated WASD walk from the pole to nearly the opposite pole.

**Body orientation**: rebuilding `global_transform.basis` every physics frame via `Basis.looking_at(tangent_forward, up)` — where `tangent_forward` is the *current* forward direction projected flat onto the plane perpendicular to the new `up` (falls back to the current right axis if forward is degenerate, i.e. looking almost exactly along up) — keeps the character's facing continuous as it walks across curvature, with no jarring snap. This has to run before computing `move_dir = transform.basis * Vector3(input.x, 0, input.y)` each frame so that basis-relative WASD movement stays tangent to the surface for free, with no separate "project onto sphere" step needed for movement itself.

## Generalizing a flat-world top-down build camera to a curved surface: snapshot a tangent frame once, don't touch it while frozen

The build-mode top-down camera/ghost-placement system (`_screen_to_build_plane`, `_is_build_position_valid`, `_build_camera_world_transform`, pan directions) originally hardcoded `Vector3.UP`/world-Y-plane assumptions throughout. Since the player is already fully frozen for the entire duration build mode is active (existing invariant, see the build-tool entry above), snapshotting `_build_up = up_direction` plus a tangent `_build_ref_forward`/`_build_ref_right` pair *once* in `_enter_build_mode()` (via the same forward-projection technique as body realignment above) and swapping every subsequent `Vector3.UP`/world-X/world-Z reference for those three vectors was sufficient — no per-frame recomputation needed, since the snapshot can't go stale while the player can't move. `Basis(_build_up, yaw)` (Godot's axis-angle `Basis` constructor accepts any axis, not just world axes) rotates the snapshotted forward/right around the local "up" for orbit-camera yaw, staying tangent automatically. The ray/plane intersection for cursor→world ghost placement generalizes from "solve for a fixed world-Y height" to "solve for the plane through `_build_focus` with normal `_build_up`" via the standard `t = (plane_point - ray_from).dot(normal) / ray_dir.dot(normal)` — same formula, just no longer assuming the normal is `(0,1,0)`.

## Driving real input in a headless integration test via `Input.parse_input_event`

`Input.is_physical_key_pressed()` reflects the `Input` singleton's internal key-state table, which is updated by *any* `InputEventKey` that passes through `Input.parse_input_event()` — not just real OS events. Constructing one (`physical_keycode = KEY_D`, `pressed = true`) and calling `Input.parse_input_event(ev)` once makes `is_physical_key_pressed(KEY_D)` return true on every subsequent frame until a matching `pressed = false` event is parsed, exactly like a real held key — no engine-internal API needed, works from a plain test script. Used to drive `Player._physics_process`'s WASD movement through many real physics ticks in a driven headless test (see mistakes.md) rather than teleporting, which produced a streaming-artifact false failure.

## Godot UI gotchas worth defaulting to

- Any `Button`/`Control` that's part of a click-driven grid (not a keyboard-navigable form) should get `focus_mode = Control.FOCUS_NONE` by default — otherwise it silently eats Tab/arrow-key input meant for gameplay. See `mistakes.md`.
- Prefer declaring a node's group membership statically in the `.tscn` (`groups=[...]` on the `[node]` line) over calling `add_to_group()` in that node's own `_ready()`, *if* anything else might look that node up during its own `_ready()` — static declaration is available immediately on instantiation, `add_to_group()` isn't available until that node's `_ready()` has actually run, which is order-dependent on sibling position in the scene tree.

## Per-planet sky/sun/fog via a `PlanetAtmosphere` Resource + a `shader_type sky;` shader built entirely in code

Same "build it in GDScript, don't hand-author it in .tscn/.tres" convention as the terrain generator/mesher/format (see above): `WorldEnvironment` is added to `world.tscn` as a bare node with no sub-resources, and `world.gd`'s `_apply_atmosphere()` constructs the `ShaderMaterial`/`Sky`/`Environment` at `_ready()` from a `PlanetAtmosphere` resource (`scripts/world/planet_atmosphere.gd`, plain `Resource` with `@export`ed colors/floats for sky gradient, sun disc/glow, directional light, fog). Modularity for "a different planet should look different" comes entirely from swapping which `.tres` the `atmosphere` export field points at — zero shader or scene changes needed for a new planet, just new numbers (`data/atmospheres/default_planet.tres` is the first one).

**Sky shader basics that worked first try**: `shader_type sky;`, write `COLOR` in `void sky()`. `EYEDIR` (view direction) `.y` component directly gives "how far above/below the local horizon this sky pixel is" for a simple `mix(horizon_color, zenith_color, pow(clamp(EYEDIR.y,0,1), 1/curve))` gradient (and the mirror image below the horizon for a "ground haze" fallback color) — no need for spherical coordinates. `LIGHT0_DIRECTION`/`LIGHT0_ENERGY` are automatically populated from the scene's `DirectionalLight3D` (no manual wiring needed beyond having one in the scene) and point *toward* the light, so `dot(EYEDIR, LIGHT0_DIRECTION)` peaks at 1.0 when looking straight at the sun - `pow(that, glow_exponent) * intensity` gives a soft glow, and `smoothstep(cos(angular_size), cos(angular_size*0.9), that)` gives a small sharp disc on top. Verified via a driven headless test that every `ShaderMaterial.get_shader_parameter()` read back exactly what the `PlanetAtmosphere` resource specified, and that `DirectionalLight3D.light_color`/`light_energy` and `Environment.fog_*` were applied from the same resource — headless can't confirm the shader *renders* correctly (no real GPU device under `--headless`), so the visual result still needs the user's actual playtest, but the whole data-plumbing path is confirmed correct.

## Foundations: an artificial local grid on a curved planet, instead of trying to force a global one

Buildings placed directly on the sphere always orient tangent to *their own* placement point (see the "radial planet gravity" entry above) - correct individually, but two independently-tangent-oriented buildings a few meters apart are each flat on their own tiny patch of a curved sphere, so they don't necessarily line up square with each other, and there's no shared axis to call "the grid" the way flat-world axis-aligned building games have. **Foundations** (`scripts/world/foundation.gd`, `Foundation.SIZE=2.0` flat plates) solve this the way Space Engineers does: a foundation snapped next to an existing one *copies that neighbor's exact `Basis`* (not its own freshly-computed tangent basis) and offsets by exactly one plate-width along the neighbor's own local right/forward axis (`player.gd`'s `_foundation_snap_transform()`). Every foundation in one connected chain ends up sharing bit-identical orientation, so the whole cluster reads as one flat, square local grid regardless of how much the sphere curves underneath it end to end - confirmed via a driven test: a snapped foundation's basis.x/basis.y dot its neighbor's at exactly `1.0000`, offset at exactly `Foundation.SIZE`.

**Three-tier placement resolution** (`player.gd`'s `_resolve_build_placement()`, single source of truth used by both the live ghost preview and the actual commit in `_place_building()`, so they can never disagree): a `Foundation` item snaps to a nearby foundation's edge if one exists, else free-places tangent to the raw terrain at the cursor (an ordinary new "island" start). A *non*-foundation building snaps to mount flush on a nearby foundation's top face (inheriting its orientation, with the player's R-key rotation still free to spin the building's own facing on top of that - matters for buildings with input/output sides) if one exists, else free-places tangent directly on raw terrain - the deliberate fallback for players who don't want to bother with foundations. Foundation's own origin convention is its *center* (unlike `Building`'s base-anchored origin), so free-foundation-placement shifts down by half its thickness from the raycast surface point so the *top* face lands flush at the cursor, not the center.

**Ghost orientation must be recomputed from the cursor's actual position every frame, not the build-session's entry-point snapshot** - reusing the session-wide `_build_up`/`_build_ref_forward` (used for the build *camera*, which is legitimately frozen for the whole session since the player doesn't move) for ghost/building placement would have reintroduced a milder version of the same "sideways" bug once the camera pans far enough from where build mode was entered for the sphere to have visibly curved in between. `_free_tangent_transform()` instead derives up fresh from `(cursor_pos - planet_center)` and forward from the *camera's current* facing (continuously updated by panning) every single frame.

## Turret buildings: `Building` subclass overrides `can_accept_input`/`can_add_output` for a non-recipe inventory use, group+duck-typing for targeting

First Slice-2 combat piece (`scripts/world/turret.gd`, `class_name Turret extends Building`), and the first consumer of the previously-inert `iron_piercing_ammo` chain. Two buildings (`scenes/turret_gun.tscn` fast/single-target, `scenes/turret_cannon.tscn` slow/splash), same script, stats set per-scene via `@export` — same "data, not subclassing" pattern `Recipe`/`Item` already use elsewhere, rather than a `TurretGun`/`TurretCannon` script pair.

**Ammo storage without a Recipe**: `Building.can_accept_input()`/`can_add_output()` gate on whether an item appears as some `Recipe`'s input/output - turrets have no recipes (firing is a per-shot ammo debit in `_physics_process`, not a crafting timer), so both are overridden directly (`can_accept_input` checks `item_id == ammo_item_id`, `can_add_output` always `false`). Because `building_ui.gd` is fully generic over any `Building` (inventory grid size, shift-click quick-transfer, drag-drop all just call `can_accept_input`/`get_inventory_count`/`remove_item`), a turret's ammo box works in the existing building UI with **zero UI code changes** - same "preserve the interface, only change what's exported" payoff as the earlier slot-based-inventory rewrite (above).

**Targeting via group membership + duck-typing, not a typed `Enemy` class**: `_find_target()` scans `get_tree().get_nodes_in_group("enemy")`, filtering on `node is Node3D and node.has_method("take_damage")`. No `class_name Enemy` dependency at all - anything that joins the `"enemy"` group and implements `take_damage(amount)` is a valid target, including the minimal `EnemyDummy` test scaffold added alongside it (a stand-in for the real Slice-2 wave/AI system, which doesn't exist yet - just health + `take_damage` + group membership, for turrets to have something real to shoot during development and in driven tests).

**Aiming survives arbitrary building orientation for free**: `_aim_at()` derives its "up" hint from `global_transform.basis.y` (the turret's own actual up, whatever the surface-tangent placement system - see the Foundations entry above - set it to) rather than `Vector3.UP`, and rotates a child `Head` node via `Transform3D.looking_at()` + `Basis.slerp()` for smooth turning. Since the barrel mesh's own local orientation is baked into its transform *relative to* `Head`, this works correctly regardless of which way the base building ended up tilted on the sphere - confirmed by construction, not by testing every orientation.

**Verified end-to-end with a driven headless test** (spawn turret + `EnemyDummy` via a temporary main-scene wrapper - same pattern as the build-tool/radial-gravity tests above): in-range dummy dies to exactly the expected shot count and ammo debit, out-of-range dummy is never targeted, zero-ammo turret never fires despite a valid target, cannon splash damages a second nearby dummy, and `can_accept_input` accepts only the configured ammo item. Caught two real bugs this way before either could reach a playtest: a freed-object/typed-parameter GDScript pitfall (see `mistakes.md`) and the already-documented `--quit-after` frame-vs-real-time trap (mistakes.md, 2026-09-03) — the test's own `get_tree().quit()` plus a deliberately huge `--quit-after` backstop is the fix, not a "reasonable-looking" frame count.

**`FoundationNetwork` is a flat `Array` + linear nearest-neighbor scan, not a spatial dictionary like `PipeNetwork`** - deliberately: `PipeNetwork`'s `Vector3i(floor(pos))` cell-keyed dictionary only works because every pipe/building is forced onto the *world axis-aligned* voxel grid. Foundations are explicitly NOT axis-aligned (that's the whole point - they follow their own local grid's orientation), so there's no fixed cell size to bucket them by. A hobby-scale base has at most dozens of foundations, so a linear `distance_to()` scan per placement-frame is simpler and correct at every orientation, where a dictionary approach would have needed a genuinely different (and more complex) spatial structure to handle arbitrary rotation.

## Real ballistic projectiles: hand-integrated gravity+drag beats a RigidBody3D for this

Converting turrets from hitscan to "actual bullets with trajectory" (`scripts/world/projectile.gd`) used a plain `Node3D` moved by hand-integrated semi-implicit Euler physics each `_physics_process` tick (`velocity += (gravity_accel + drag_accel) * delta; position += velocity * delta`, gravity from `PlanetPhysics.get_gravity_accel()`, drag from the standard quadratic-drag formula `0.5 * air_density * Cd * cross_section_area * speed^2`, divided by mass for acceleration) rather than a real `RigidBody3D`. Reasoning: Godot's built-in gravity is a single global world-space vector by default, wrong for a planet with radial gravity, so a rigid body would need the exact same manual per-tick force override anyway - the "real physics engine" wouldn't actually save the hard part. Hand-integrating also makes hit detection trivial and reliable for a fast-moving small object: sweep a `PhysicsRayQueryParameters3D` from the previous tick's position to the new one every tick (not a discrete point-in-time check), which can't tunnel through a thin target in one step the way naive collision could. Verified via a driven test that directly re-derives the expected velocity/position after one integration step by hand in the test script itself and compares against the projectile's actual values (sub-centimeter tolerance) - a much stronger check than "did it eventually hit something," and confirmed separately that a projectile's path visibly curves away from a straight line after ~0.5s of flight (gravity is really being applied, not just present in the formula).

**One shared `PlanetPhysics` autoload for gravity/planet-center/air-density, not three separate copies**: player movement, turret aiming, and projectile flight all need "where's the planet center" and "how strong is gravity" - previously player.gd had its own private copy of both. Extracted into `scripts/planet_physics.gd` (autoload) and migrated player.gd onto it too rather than letting a second copy exist, specifically because a future change to planet gravity/scale only needs to happen once. `air_density` lives here too, set once by `world.gd`'s `_apply_atmosphere()` from the active `PlanetAtmosphere` resource - a different planet's thinner/thicker atmosphere changes projectile drag automatically with zero extra wiring, matching the "influenced by atmosphere" ask directly.

**Ammo type owns its own complete ballistics+combat spec, not the turret** (`scripts/data/ammo_type.gd`, a plain `Resource`: mass/muzzle_velocity/caliber/drag_coefficient plus damage/splash): tried the alternative (turret owns damage/splash, ammo is just a string id to consume) first and it meant two turrets sharing one ammo item couldn't have different combat stats *because* of the ammo, only because of which turret fired it - backwards from how "not every turret should use every ammo type" is supposed to be a meaningful choice. Making `AmmoType` fully self-contained means a turret's `compatible_ammo: Array[AmmoType]` list is the *entire* story for what it can do and how - a new weapon variant is a new ammo type's numbers, not new turret code.

**Turret firing solution (ballistic aim) is a deliberately different, simpler model than the projectile's actual simulated flight**: `turret.gd`'s `_compute_aim_point()` solves the classic no-drag projectile-range quadratic for launch angle (`tan_theta = (v² - sqrt(v⁴ - g(gx² + 2yv²))) / (gx)`, low-arc root) purely to decide which way to point the barrel, while the round that actually gets fired flies with full drag simulation in `projectile.gd`. These two models disagreeing slightly (the firing solution slightly over/under-elevates vs. what a drag-affected round actually needs) is fine and expected - a real gunnery computer's firing solution and a round's real flight path aren't identical either. Falls back to aiming flat at the target when the quadratic's discriminant is negative (target is beyond what this ammo's muzzle velocity can ballistically reach at all, even though it's within the turret's `attack_range` sensor/targeting radius) rather than clamping to some arbitrary max angle - an honest "this shot will fall short," not a hidden fudge.

**Verified via a driven headless test**: hand-recomputing the expected post-integration velocity/position and comparing to the real projectile (Test A), a turret auto-firing at an in-range ammo-stocked target actually damaging it after the projectile has flight time to arrive (Test B, not instant), `set_ammo_enabled(false)` genuinely stopping fire despite a full magazine (Test C), a multi-ammo turret preferring its first-declared compatible/enabled/stocked type over a second one (Test D), and `building_ui.gd`'s new ammo-toggle row mirroring/round-tripping a turret's real `compatible_ammo`/`_enabled_ammo` state (Test E). Caught a real runtime type-error this way (`mistakes.md`) that a plain parse/boot smoke test would never have exercised, since it only threw once a real projectile actually flew far enough to hit its per-tick raycast branch.

## `SlotCarry`: one click-to-carry/place/merge/swap implementation, driven through duck-typed container adapters

Before this, "pick up an item stack from a slot, place it in another, merging or swapping as needed" had three independent hand-written copies: `inventory_ui.gd`'s player grid+hands, and two more *inside* `building_ui.gd` (the building's own inventory grid, and the modifier slots). They'd already drifted from each other - `inventory_ui.gd` let you swap two different item stacks between slots; `building_ui.gd`'s building-slot version silently refused that exact case; carried-item labels showed the item's real display name in one screen and the raw internal id in another. Consolidated into `scripts/ui/slot_carry.gd` (`class_name SlotCarry extends RefCounted`, one `click(container, index)` method containing the entire pick-up/merge/swap state machine) plus three tiny adapters wrapping each real backing store behind one duck-typed contract (`get_slot`/`set_slot`/`can_accept_at`/`get_room`/`get_max_stack`): `inventory_slot_adapter.gd` (the player's `Inventory` autoload, one `Kind` enum value each for the grid vs. the 2 hand slots), `building_inventory_adapter.gd` (any `Building`'s stacked inventory - works unchanged for Chest/Constructor/Turret's ammo box/etc. since they already all share `Building`'s storage), `building_modifier_adapter.gd` (a `Building`'s single-item modifier slots, reported as `max_stack=1` so SlotCarry's ordinary merge logic handles "no stacking" for free with zero modifier-specific code in SlotCarry itself). Both UI scripts now just instantiate the adapters that apply to them and call `_carry.click(adapter, index)` from each button's handler - `inventory_ui.gd` and `building_ui.gd`'s player-grid section literally share the *same* `InventorySlotAdapter` class now, not just similar-looking code.

**`get_room(item_id) -> int` as a separate concept from `can_accept_at(index, item_id, amount)`** is what let the generic merge logic correctly reproduce the original "always place as much as fits" partial-placement behavior for capacity-limited containers (Building) while also working correctly for containers with no capacity concept at all (the player's Inventory, modifier slots - just return a large sentinel). `BuildingInventoryAdapter.get_room()` probes `building.can_accept_input(item_id, 0)` specifically to isolate "is this item type even accepted here" from the capacity arithmetic, since every `can_accept_input` override in this codebase (base `Building`, `Turret`, `Chest`) already folds both into one bool - amount=0 makes the capacity half of that check trivially true, leaving only the type-compatibility half.

**Verified via a driven test that specifically re-created the drift**: swapping two different item stacks now works identically whether the target is the player's grid or a `Building`'s inventory (previously only worked for the former); a capacity-limited `Building` still correctly place-and-clamps a too-large carried stack and keeps the remainder carried (not silently dropped); a modifier slot placement/pickup goes through the exact same code path as every other slot (verified against a building temporarily given a `compatible_modifiers` entry, since no real modifier items exist as content yet); the pre-existing ammo-toggle row (see the ballistics entry above) still works unchanged after the `building_ui.gd` rewrite around it.

## Multi-planet, phase 1: `CelestialBody` (data) + `Planet` (scene) + a real multi-body `PlanetPhysics` registry, proven with two simultaneous planets before touching gameplay

Generalized "the game has exactly one planet" (explicit user direction 2026-09-04, see CLAUDE.md's Architecture principles) into a real multi-body foundation, staged deliberately narrow: this is data/physics-layer readiness, not the star-system generator, ship, or save system (all separately scoped, not started).

**`CelestialBody`** (`scripts/world/celestial_body.gd`, plain `Resource`): everything that used to be hardcoded `const`s in `ore_generator.gd` (radius, noise amplitudes, per-ore rarity thresholds, seed) plus a `PlanetAtmosphere` reference - a planet is now data, the same way `Recipe`/`Item`/`AmmoType` already are elsewhere in this project. `ore_generator.gd` takes one in `_init(body: CelestialBody = null)` (default-null so the `@tool` editor-preview instantiation path and isolated tests still work without a real body) and every noise's seed derives from one `base_seed` with fixed per-noise offsets, so one number gives a whole planet a distinct-but-reproducible terrain/ore pattern.

**`Planet`** (`scripts/world/planet.gd` + `scenes/planet.tscn`): owns a `VoxelTerrain` child, builds the generator/format/mesher/bounds from its own `body` export at `_ready()` (bounds read straight from `body`, not a separate hand-kept constant, so they can't drift out of sync with what the generator is actually shaping), and registers/unregisters itself with `PlanetPhysics`. A second planet in the game is a second `Planet` instance with a different `CelestialBody` at a different `Transform3D` in `world.tscn` - zero new code. `world.gd` no longer owns a `Terrain` at all; it's now the shared "universe" root holding just the one `DirectionalLight3D` (the system's star - a per-planet sun isn't meaningful without orbital mechanics, which don't exist) and one `WorldEnvironment`, watching `PlanetPhysics.get_nearest_body(player.global_position)` every frame and reapplying `_apply_atmosphere()` only when the nearest body's atmosphere actually *changes* - so flying from one planet's sky into another's happens with no scene reload and no per-frame rebuild while sitting still on one.

**`PlanetPhysics` is a real registry now** (`register_body`/`unregister_body`/`get_nearest_body`, all consumers - `get_up`/`get_gravity_accel`/`get_air_density`/`get_planet_center`/`get_nearest_terrain` - resolving through it): every one of them correctly falls through to a "no body in range" case (`Vector3.UP` fallback direction, zero gravity, zero/vacuum air density) rather than assuming a planet always exists. The one subtlety that isn't obvious from "just pick whichever body is closest": **without a real cutoff, gravity never actually reaches zero** - "closest" is still defined even when every registered body is enormously far away, so a naive nearest-wins query would keep attributing full gravity to whichever planet happens to be marginally less distant no matter how far that still is. `Planet.influence_radius()` (a simple multiple of the body's own radius - `4x` for now, no real orbital-mechanics need yet) gives each body a bounded gravity/atmosphere well; `get_nearest_body()` only returns a body the query position is actually inside that well for, so two planets placed far enough apart (tested at a 500m gap against ~40m/24m-radius bodies, each with a ~160m/~96m well - a wide true-void margin) have a genuine zero-gravity, vacuum gap between them.

**Player's own terrain reference had to stop being a one-time cached lookup** (`_terrain = get_tree().get_first_node_in_group("voxel_terrain")` in `_ready()`, from the original single-planet build) and become a per-physics-frame `PlanetPhysics.get_nearest_terrain(global_position)` re-resolve - the exact same "cached singleton assumption" bug `PlanetPhysics` itself had, just living in a different file. Also swapped player.gd's raw `PlanetPhysics.GRAVITY_MAGNITUDE * delta` gravity integration for `PlanetPhysics.get_gravity_accel(pos).length() * delta` (same value near a planet, correctly zero in open space) - not reachable in normal play yet (no way off a planet without the not-yet-built ship/jetpack), but correct from day one rather than needing a second fix once it is.

**Verified via a driven test with two real, simultaneously-registered planets** (a ~40m-world-radius "Home" and a ~24m-radius vacuum "Test Moon" 500 world-meters away): each resolves gravity/up/terrain/atmosphere to *itself* at its own surface (not the other body's), the moon's `air_density=0` atmosphere is genuinely per-body (a projectile there would correctly feel zero drag), the midpoint between them resolves to no body at all with exactly zero gravity and zero air density, and - the regression check that mattered most - the player still spawns and settles correctly on the home planet exactly as before this refactor, and `world.gd`'s shared sky correctly ends up showing the home planet's atmosphere while standing on it.

**Not done, deliberately deferred** (see CLAUDE.md/gamebrief.md): a real procedural star-system generator (tonight's two bodies are hand-placed `.tres` files, not generated); floating-origin/precision handling for genuinely large distances (500m is nowhere near where Godot's single-precision floats start to matter - defer until real inter-planet distances are decided); a ship/vehicle controller and oxygen/fuel mechanics for actually reaching the void; save/persistence (registering/unregistering with `PlanetPhysics` is purely a runtime, in-memory thing right now - nothing about a planet's dug terrain or built structures survives past the current process).
